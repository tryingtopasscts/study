Part A — Hard Numbers, Formulas & Standards

The priority set — flagged as not studied at all until the AV Math Guide was expanded to cover it.

Electrical

Unit of currentAmperes (not watts — watts is power, P = V×I)
Opposition in an AC circuitImpedance (resistance is the DC-only term)
Ohm's Law, solved for currentI = V / R
Power path from the streetService entrance → main panel → feeders → distribution panels → branch circuits

RF / Wireless

RF spectrum range3 kHz to 300 GHz — has a floor, not just a ceiling

Video / Light

ANSI lumens formulaL = Screen Height × Screen Width × Average Illuminance
Throw distance formulaThrow distance = Throw ratio × Image width (e.g., 2.0 × 4 m = 8 m)

Audio Math

Part B — Terms & Concepts to Re-Anchor

Vocabulary that's already half-known — these need re-anchoring, not new learning.

ConfusionThe distinction
Analog vs. digitalAnalog = many/continuously varying states. Digital = discrete states.
Signal chain endpointThe output device (e.g., loudspeaker) converts signal to acoustic energy — a processor doesn't.
Video bandwidth measurement toolSpectrum analyzer ("bandwidth monitor" isn't a real instrument)
Bus vs. mesh topologyBus = one linear line. Mesh = fully interconnected, redundant paths.
802.11 vs. 802.1X802.11 = Wi-Fi. 802.1X = port-based access control. Similar numbers, unrelated standards.
Fiber jacket colorsYellow = single-mode (longest reach). Orange = multimode (shorter reach).
Router vs. gatewayGateway = specifically connects to an outside network.
Cable vs. wireCable = multiple conductors bundled. Shielding is unrelated to the distinction.
Function vs. programFunction = one action, one device. Program = coordinated multi-step/multi-device sequence.
Task parameters' purposeFeed general AV needs/requirements — not exact equipment model selection.
Reflected ceiling planShows sprinklers/HVAC/lighting for ceiling coordination — not an elevation drawing.
Ambient light controlLighter wall finishes hurt (reflect more light); focused task lighting helps (a valid reduction strategy).
Building codes' scope on AVDictate wiring type + mounting/structural rules — not the AV system's functional layout.
Audio frequency response specMatch it to the application — not "widest the budget allows."
IDC termination8P8C/RJ-45 = insulation displacement. BNC is a different (twist-lock) mechanism.
Rack cooling typesEvacuation = fan-driven exhaust. Convection = passive, no fans.
"Striking" an eventTearing down the temporary system after use — not testing audio.
WBS vs. logic networkWBS = nouns (deliverables, "what"). Logic network = verbs (activities: build, test, fabricate).
RFI → RFC → CO chainRFI = ask a question. RFC = propose a change. Approved RFC becomes a CO (change order). CCD/field order = a separate emergency bypass of that whole chain.
Equipment security responsibilityLies with whoever currently has control of the equipment — not "the AV company, always."
Manufacturer's warranty vs. service agreementManufacturer's warranty = the equipment itself, via an authorized service center. Service agreement = the AV company's broader ongoing-maintenance commitment.
What a manufacturer's warranty excludesRemoval/reinstallation of installed gear and shipping to the service center — typically not covered.
Firmware/software update timingOnly when it improves performance/security/reliability or adds required functionality — not "the moment it's released." Updates can cascade into unexpected results elsewhere.
Fault-localization efficiencyDivide the system in half, test, repeat — more efficient than testing backward from the output.
Laptop dual-display on batterySome laptops won't drive both the built-in screen and an external output on battery alone — plug into mains.
Feedback-control best practicesMic close to the source helps. Adding compression to the mic chain is not a standard feedback-prevention technique.
"Waterfall" hiss causePoor gain structure (inputs too low, later stages over-gained to compensate) — not a blown tweeter.
Recurring Pattern Two separate misses (the 1:50 drawing-scale conversion, and the inverse square law calc) were the same mistake in different clothes: multiplying when the formula calls for a ratio. Before plugging numbers into any formula question, check whether the relationship is proportional (divide) or compound (multiply).
