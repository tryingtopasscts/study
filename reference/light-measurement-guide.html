Where you point the meter
changes what you measure:
Incident vs. Reflected

Every unit below describes the same light at a different point in its journey — leaving the source, landing on a surface, or bouncing off that surface toward your eye. Follow the beam.

Gold units describe light on its way in (incident). The cyan units describe light on its way back out (reflected / emitted) — what a viewer actually perceives as "brightness."

Direct · Incident

Light arriving somewhere

These four units follow light from the source to the surface. Think of them as a garden hose: the nozzle's strength, the total water flowing, and how wet the ground gets.

SI BASE
Candelacd

Luminous intensity — how strong the light is in a single direction. The SI base unit everything else is built from.

Hose analogy: the pressure at the nozzle, aimed one way. One ordinary candle ≈ 1 cd — that's literally where the name comes from.

1 cd radiating in all directions = 4π ≈ 12.57 lumens
SI
Lumenlm

Luminous flux — the quantity of light a source emits. Formally: the photons from a 1 cd source passing through 1 m² of a sphere 1 m in radius around it. This is the spec on every projector and bulb box.

Hose analogy: total gallons per minute leaving the hose — regardless of where it's aimed. Projector specs usually cite ANSI lumens — the most commonly quoted measurement method worldwide, even though it's no longer the official international standard.

1 lm = 1 cd through 1 m² @ 1 m radius · full sphere = 12.57 lm
SI
Luxlx

Illuminance — lumens landing per square meter of surface. Measured at the task area: the desk, the podium, the screen face.

Hose analogy: how wet one square meter of the garden gets. Same hose, bigger garden = drier soil. Same lumens, bigger screen = fewer lux.

1 lux = 1 lumen / m²
US
Foot-candlefc

The US customary version of lux — lumens landing per square foot. Historically: the illuminance one candle throws on a surface one foot away.

Key intuition: a square foot is ~10.76× smaller than a square meter, so the same light packed into it reads ~10.76× more concentrated.

1 fc = 1 lm/ft² ≈ 10.76 lux
Reflected · Luminance

Light coming back at you

Once light hits a screen or wall, some of it bounces back toward the viewer. Luminance is the brightness you actually see — it depends on both the light arriving and how reflective (or emissive) the surface is.

SI
Nitcd/m²

Luminance — candelas of intensity per square meter of surface, headed toward the viewer. TVs, phones, and displays are all specced in nits.

Why it matters: nits describe emitting and reflecting surfaces alike — a 600-nit TV and a projection screen bouncing 48 nits are measured the exact same way.

1 nit = 1 cd/m²
US
Foot-lambertfL

The US customary luminance unit. Defined so the math is friendly: a perfectly diffuse white surface lit by 1 foot-candle reflects exactly 1 foot-lambert.

Cinema staple: SMPTE's classic target for a dark-room projection screen is ~16 fL open-gate (~48 nits). You'll see fL constantly in projection specs.

1 fL = (1/π) cd/ft² ≈ 3.426 cd/m² (nits)
Tools

Two meters, two questions

The physical distinction is simple: which way is the sensor facing?

Incident meter

The dome faces the light source. It answers: "How much light is arriving here?" You hold it at the task area — the desk, the lectern, the screen surface — pointed back toward the room/projector.

Reads → lux · foot-candles

Reflected / spot meter

The lens faces the surface. It answers: "How bright does that surface look?" You aim it at the screen or wall from the viewing position, like a camera. Factors in the surface's reflectance or emission.

Reads → nits (cd/m²) · foot-lamberts
Cheat Sheet

The three conversions worth memorizing

Illuminance · US ↔ SI
1 fc ≈ 10.76 lux
1 lux ≈ 0.093 fc

Because 1 m² ≈ 10.76 ft². Quick field math: fc × 10 ≈ lux.

Luminance · US ↔ SI
1 fL ≈ 3.43 nits

The π in the definition is what makes this 3.43, not 10.76.

Source · Intensity ↔ Flux
1 cd = 12.57 lm

Only if radiating uniformly in all directions (a full 4π sphere).

Physics

The inverse square law

Light radiating from a point source spreads out as it travels — the same lumens cover more and more area, so the illuminance at any surface drops with the square of the distance. Same principle as sound, and any energy radiating from a point.

The same total light (lumens) spreads over an area that grows with distance² — so illuminance (lux / fc) shrinks by 1 / distance².

Moving away
×2 dist → ¼
×3 dist → 1/9
×5 dist → 1/25

Divide brightness by the distance factor squared.

Moving closer
÷2 dist → 4×
÷3 dist → 9×
÷5 dist → 25×

Multiply brightness by the distance factor squared.

The formula
E ∝ 1 / d²

Illuminance changes with the inverse of the square of the distance from the source.

Quick Test

Which unit do I need?

Ask yourself: am I measuring light going toward a surface, or coming off one?

Toward a surface → Incident

  • Spec'ing a projector? → lumens
  • How strong in one direction? → candela
  • Enough light on the desk / task? → lux or fc
  • Meter dome faces the source

Off a surface → Reflected

  • How bright does the screen look? → nits or fL
  • Display / TV spec? → nits
  • Projection screen target? → fL
  • Spot meter lens faces the surface
