PART II · CHAPTERS 3–10

Essentials of AV Technology

Signals, audio, video, networks, signal management, control, electrical, wireless. Foundational — doesn't map to exam duties directly, but everything in Parts III–V assumes it.

Hard Numbers & Specs

Each row: the fact to memorize, then what it actually means in the room. If a value here surprises you, that's the row to flashcard.

SPECVALUEWHAT IT MEANS
Human hearing20 Hz – 20 kHzThe audible range for a healthy ear. Audio systems are designed around this bandwidth, and all the sampling math below starts from the 20 kHz ceiling.
Nyquist–ShannonSample at more than 2× the highest frequencyTo digitize a signal you can later reconstruct, sample faster than double its top frequency. Capturing 20 kHz audio needs >40 kHz sampling — which is exactly why CD audio landed at 44.1 kHz.
CD audio44.1 kHz / 16-bit ≈ 96 dB dynamic range16 bits = 65,536 discrete levels, ≈ 96 dB of range — covers most of human hearing. Rounding an analog voltage to the nearest level creates quantization error, audible as low-level noise.
Pro audio sampling48 / 96 / 192 kHz · 24-bit ≈ 144 dB · 32-bit ≈ 192 dBRecording and live production use higher rates to capture overtones, and deeper bit depths to keep quantization noise down while mixing multiple digital signals together.
dB references0 dB SPL = 20 µPa · 0 dBu = 775 mV · 0 dBV = 1.0 V · 0 dBm = 1 mWA decibel is a ratio, not a quantity — "85 dB" is meaningless until you know 85 dB relative to what. SPL references the threshold of hearing. Note: −20 dBu isn't negative voltage, just a voltage below 775 mV.
Doubling distance−6 dB, every timeDouble the distance from a source and the energy spreads over 4× the area, so level drops 6 dB. Identical physics for loudspeakers and light fixtures — the inverse square law, diagrammed below.
Pro mic impedanceLow-Z ≤ 200 Ω · high-Z > 25 kΩProfessional mics are low impedance: they pick up less noise and support much longer cable runs. High-impedance mics are consumer-grade and distance-limited.
Impedance bridgingOutput ≤ 1/10 of the input it feedsModern audio transfers voltage, not power. A device's output impedance should be at most one-tenth of the input impedance downstream — e.g., a ≤200 Ω mic feeding a ≥2 kΩ input.
Speaker sensitivitydB SPL @ 1 W @ 1 mHow loud the speaker gets from 1 watt, measured 1 meter away. It's the anchor of every amplifier power calc: start here, subtract distance loss (20-log), add headroom.
Coax impedance75 Ω = video · 50 Ω = RF/dataImpedance comes from the cable's physical construction (conductor spacing, dielectric), so these are genuinely different cables — never interchangeable, even though they look alike.
Fiber jacketsSingle-mode = yellow · multimode = orangeSingle-mode carries one light path and reaches the farthest distances; multimode carries many paths over shorter runs. In the field, the jacket color is the ID.
Aspect ratios16:9 = 1.7778 · 4:3 = 1.3333 · computers often 16:10Width ÷ height. Portrait-mounting a display flips the ratio (16:10 becomes 10:16) — always check the spec. Carry four decimals through calculations and round only at the end.
Illuminance1 fc = 10.76 luxBoth measure light falling on a surface: foot-candles are lumens per square foot, lux are lumens per square meter. Same light, different floor unit — multiply fc by 10.76 to get lux.
IEEE 802.11The Wi-Fi standard familyConnection speed is tied to signal strength: as strength weakens, the link slows down (it doesn't just drop). User count on the access point also drags speed.
IPv6 address8 groups of 4 hex digitsVersus IPv4's four decimal octets. Recognize the format on sight — it's a recall question.
Manual IP configIP address + subnet mask (minimum)Those two are required to configure a device by hand. Otherwise a DHCP server assigns addressing automatically when the device joins the network.
Ethernet termination8P8C connector · T568-B pinout dominant8P8C is what everyone calls "RJ-45." Nearly all installs use the T568-B wiring format; terminate A on one end and B on the other and you've made a crossover cable — two devices can link without a switch.
RS-485 / DMX512Balanced · 32 devices · 120 Ω both ends · 5-pin XLRThe serial standard under DMX512 lighting control: a balanced line daisy-chaining up to 32 devices, requiring 120 Ω terminating resistors at both ends of the run.
GFCI / RCD trip time< 1/10 secondGround-fault devices sense minuscule current leaking to earth and cut the supply in under a tenth of a second. Widely used, and code-mandated in many jurisdictions.
Circuit loadingPlan to ≤ 80% of capacityUniversal electrical design rule, permanent or temporary installs alike: never plan a circuit to carry more than 80% of its rating.

Formulas

dB = 10 × log(P₁/P₂)Power ratio. Ex: 2× power = 10 log(2) = +3 dB
dB = 20 × log(V₁/V₂)  ·  dB = 20 × log(D₁/D₂)Voltage and distance use 20-log (quadratic relationships). Ex: doubling distance = 20 log(1/2) = −6 dB
λ = v / fWavelength = speed of sound ÷ frequency. Ex: 343 ÷ 1000 Hz ≈ 0.34 m
V = I × R  ·  P = V × IOhm's law + power. Ex: 2 A through 8 Ω → V = 16 V, P = 32 W
AR = W / H  ·  A² + B² = C²Aspect ratio; screen diagonal via Pythagoras. Ex: 16:9 screen 4 ft tall → W = 4 × 1.7778 = 7.11 ft
Expected SPL = Sensitivity + 20 log(D₁/D₂)Ex: 92 dB @ 1 W @ 1 m speaker, listener at 8 m → 92 + 20 log(1/8) = 92 − 18 = 74 dB SPL
Inverse square law with a worked chain. Same math for light: double the throw distance, quarter the illuminance.

Concept Essentials

Analog vs. digital noise

Analog noise accumulates with every re-amplification. Digital circuitry decides high/low and retransmits clean — until it fails entirely.

Lossless vs. lossy

Lossless (ZIP, FLAC, PNG) reconstructs perfectly; required for text/financial data. Lossy (MP3, JPEG, H.264, HEVC) discards what humans can't perceive — fine for AV. A codec encodes and decodes.

Balanced vs. unbalanced

Balanced circuits + twisted pair reject common-mode interference → longer runs. Coax is inherently unbalanced (center = signal, shield = return).

Signal levels

Mic level (~mV) → line level (~1 V) → speaker level (~30 V+). Keep them physically separated in racks and cable runs to prevent crosstalk.

HDMI = DVI + extras

HDMI adds audio, EDID, HDCP, Ethernet to DVI (backward compatible). SDI (SMPTE, 75 Ω BNC) carries audio + control but no HDCP. HDBaseT puts HDMI + Ethernet + control + USB + 100 W power on one Cat cable.

EDID & native resolution

EDID tells the source what the display can do. Feeding a display its native resolution avoids scaling and gives the best image.

OSI model anchors

Layer 1 Physical = cabling, patchbays. Layer 7 Application. AV pros live at 1 and 7. MAC address = Layer 2; IP/routing = Layer 3.

Subnets vs. VLANs

Subnets isolate broadcast traffic (don't reduce bandwidth use). VLANs subdivide further, ignore physical topology — a device has one subnet but can join multiple VLANs. Digital signage = classic VLAN use.

Network gear roles

Unmanaged switch: plug and play. Router: moves packets between networks. Gateway: connects to outside networks. Firewall: filters by security rules. DHCP: auto-assigns IPs.

Control system types

Contact closure, variable voltage, IR (line of sight), serial digital (RS-232 unbalanced/DB-9; RS-422 balanced = longer runs), RF, and networked. Bidirectional preferred — devices confirm status.

Grounding

Safety ground = low-resistance fault path that trips breakers. Isolated/technical ground gives all gear one reference → kills ground loops (50/60 Hz hum, rolling video bars). Class II double-insulated gear (square-in-square symbol) needs no ground.

Series vs. parallel

Series: same current everywhere, voltage divides (biggest R drops most V). Parallel: same voltage everywhere, current divides (biggest R gets least I).

WATCH OUT Never use ground-lift adapters — for any reason. And never run non-AV loads (coffee makers, vacuums) on technical power circuits; they inject switching noise and can overload the circuit.

Part II — Most Tested

  • Nyquist: sample at more than twice the highest frequency
  • Digital regenerates through noise; analog degrades generation by generation
  • 10-log for power, 20-log for voltage and distance; double distance = −6 dB
  • 75 Ω = video coax, 50 Ω = RF/data; yellow fiber = single-mode = farthest
  • 1 fc = 10.76 lux; 16:9 = 1.78 (use 1.7778 in math)
  • HDMI adds audio/EDID/HDCP/Ethernet to DVI; SDI has no HDCP
  • Manual IP needs address + subnet mask; DHCP does it for you
  • Ground loops = hum + rolling bars; fixed by isolated grounding, never by ground lifts
PART III · CHAPTERS 11–16 · CREATING AV SOLUTIONS

Creating AV Solutions

The biggest exam duty area. Ch. 11 and much of 13–15 is heavily padded "professionalism" prose — the testable core below is small but dense. Scope development alone ≈ 9% of your score.

Hard Numbers & Specs

Fewer numbers here — Part III is process-heavy. But these are the ones that show up as straight recall.

SPECVALUEWHAT IT MEANS
16:9 as a decimal1.7778 — keep four decimals16 ÷ 9 = 1.7778, so HD screen width = height × 1.7778. A 4-ft-tall image is 7.11 ft wide. Round only at the final step of a multi-step problem — early rounding compounds.
Conference speech levelsTarget 70 dB SPL A · background ≤ 45 dB SPL AThe design target at the listener is 70 dB SPL (A-weighted); background noise must stay 25 dB below it so speech detail isn't masked. That 25 dB cushion is the point of the pairing.
dB weightingA = quiet listening · C = loud (> 85 dB SPL)At low levels the ear discounts bass, so A-weighting mirrors quiet-level perception (conference rooms). Above ~85 dB SPL the ear flattens out, so C-weighting fits loud environments like concerts. Match the weighting to the level.
Lighting zonesMinimum two, separately controlledPresentation spaces need the screen-area lights switchable/dimmable independently of audience lights — the screen zone goes dark while people can still take notes.
Design process standardANSI/INFOCOMM 2M-2010Defines the methods, tasks, and deliverables of the whole AV design-and-coordination process. It applies to every phase but is the governing reference for the program phase specifically.
Audio coverage standardAVIXA A102.01:2017 (ACU)Audio Coverage Uniformity: every seat should hear the same SPL. One listener at 70 dB while another strains at 60 dB = a failed system, regardless of average level.
Display sizing standardAVIXA DISCASGiven the viewing task (see ramp below), DISCAS calculations yield image size, closest and farthest viewer positions, and horizontal viewing angles. It also feeds content-design metrics.
Needs drive everything downstream. The classic wrong answer starts at the bottom — picking equipment before the need is defined.
DISCAS viewing-task categories. Exam pattern: match the space to the task (radiology reading room = ADM), then the task to the sizing rule.

Concept Essentials

Needs analysis ≠ equipment list

A needs analysis identifies what end users need to do. Best info source: end-user task descriptions, not management assumptions. Systems designed on designer assumptions alone are "frequently off the mark."

Benchmarking

Letting the client experience existing AV systems that solve similar problems, to calibrate expectations before design.

Program report

AKA concept design report / return brief / AV narrative. Contents: executive summary, space planning, systems descriptions, infrastructure, AV budgets. Objective: tell decision-makers the overall capabilities and budget. On approval it becomes the (often contractual) basis of the design phase — and sometimes the RFP.

Form follows function

Needs → applications → system functions → infrastructure. Include end users in the program phase so all parties are invested. Detailed technical info goes to clients as an electronic document, not email/phone.

Site survey math

Drawing scales, architectural symbols, area/volume, and unit conversions — the most legitimately testable slice of Ch. 12. All formulas live in the AV Math guide.

Ambient light rules image

A front-projection screen reflects all light — projected and ambient alike. Black can't be blacker than the unlit screen, so ambient light sets the contrast floor. Windows facing screens are problem #1.

Finishes & sightlines

Surfaces near the screen should be darker and neutral — light finishes bounce light into the screen. A sightline study maps lowest visible screen point, near/far viewing angles, off-axis distortion, ergonomics.

Acoustic assessment

Noise criteria ratings (NC / NR / RC) quantify background noise. Reverberation comes from hard parallel surfaces. Speech systems need level and uniformity (AVIXA ACU standard) — same SPL at every seat.

Audio stability

Feedback and distortion fixes: acoustic treatment, EQ, feedback suppression, mix-minus — plus proper mic/speaker selection and placement in the first place.

Defining a control system

Start with a blank sheet: equipment needing control on the left, required functions per device on the right. That function list is step one of communicating with the programmer. Then interoperability (control path layout) and interconnectivity.

WATCH OUT The Udemy instructor rounds 16:9 to "1.7." The correct decimal is 1.78 (1.7778). His answer happened to land on 7.11 ft, but 1.7 will not reliably reproduce correct results. Round only at the end.

Part III — Most Tested

  • Pyramid order: needs → applications → systems → infrastructure — never gear-first
  • Best needs source = end users describing tasks; program report communicates capability + budget to decision-makers
  • Viewing tasks: passive / BDM / ADM (pixel-level = medical, CAD) / full-motion
  • Ambient light sets contrast; front screens reflect everything; two lighting zones minimum
  • 70 dB SPL A target, ≤ 45 dB SPL A background for conference speech
  • A-weighting = quiet, C-weighting = loud (> 85 dB SPL)
  • ANSI/INFOCOMM 2M-2010 governs the program/design process; DISCAS governs display sizing
PART IV · CHAPTERS 17–19 · IMPLEMENTING AV SOLUTIONS

Implementing AV Solutions

Integration (racks, cable, mounting, control, network config), operation/verification, and project management. Project management alone ≈ 8% of your score.

Hard Numbers & Specs

Implementation numbers are few but concrete — these are exactly the kind of spec the exam quizzes verbatim.

SPECVALUEWHAT IT MEANS
Accessible controls380–1,220 mm (15–48 in) above floorAny control or media slot a user must reach has to sit in this band, which leaves only ~840 mm (33 in) of usable rack height for accessible gear — sometimes forcing an additional rack.
Heavy equipment placementBottom of free-standing racksWeight up high makes a rack tippable. Heavy 1–2 RU pieces may also need rear rail support — front-only mounting puts serious torque on the faceplate.
Heat managementHot gear high or separated · fill every spaceHeat rises, so the hottest equipment goes near the top or gets spacing. Every unused RU gets a blank or vent panel — an empty slot short-circuits the designed airflow path.
Verification standardANSI/InfoComm 10:2013The performance-verification (commissioning) framework: a reference list of ~150 verification items proving the installed system meets the contracted spec, with problems caught before handover.
Termination checkContinuity testAfter terminating, a continuity test confirms the conductor path end to end. Visible stray wires (or visible solder) are the telltale of a badly fabricated termination.
Change order (CO)Signed by client AND companyAny work beyond the contracted scope gets documented — the work and its cost — in a CO signed by both parties before the work happens. No handshake extras.
Building change hits AV?Submit an RFCWhen a modification to the building design impacts AV installation requirements, the AV company's document is the request for change — not a progress report, not a CCD.
The three planning artifacts, in build order. A dependency = a task that cannot start until another finishes (mount the display only after walls are painted).

Concept Essentials

Rack building

Three tasks: build the rack, install equipment, wire it. Guided by the rack-elevation drawing. Assemble in the shop (lay the rack on its back), record serial numbers before loading.

Ergonomics & signal separation

User-facing gear at easy reach; rarely-touched gear top/bottom. Group cables by signal level — mic-level (mV) physically apart from speaker-level (30 V) to prevent EMI crosstalk.

RF/IR in racks

The metal rack can shield rack-mounted receivers from their own signal — antennas must be positioned for reception.

Cable discipline

Right cable/connector per signal, gentle bends, near-minimum lengths, label everything, tie off, continuity-test before connecting. Neat inside and out.

Serial control refresher

RS-232: DB-9, unbalanced, bidirectional. RS-422: balanced → longer distances. RS-485: balanced, 32 devices, 120 Ω both ends (DMX512). Bidirectional preferred for status feedback.

Networked AV config

Subnetting, VLANs, VPN, IP addressing settled with the client's IT during design — installation is where you configure to that plan, in coordination with IT staff.

Performance verification

ANSI/InfoComm 10:2013 = the commissioning framework. Proves the system meets the contracted spec, finds issues before handover. Method: test-signal generator at the start of the path, calibrate at points along it.

AV goes in last

AV electronics install after other trades finish and the site is clean — the gear is too sensitive to dust, moisture, and temperature. But conduit/backboxes must precede cable, and cable precedes ceiling grid.

Effort vs. duration

Effort = work time in a perfect world. Duration = calendar reality (resource availability, other projects, holidays). Perfect-world estimating underbids labor.

Price ≠ cost

Client fee = cost of labor/materials + markup covering overhead (rent, insurance, benefits) and profit. Budget = allocated labor + materials for the agreed work.

Part IV — Most Tested

  • Heavy gear low, hot gear high/separated, fill every rack space for airflow
  • Signal separation = grouping by signal level to prevent crosstalk
  • Accessible interface zone: 380–1,220 mm (15–48 in)
  • Stray wires = bad termination; verify with a continuity test
  • WBS = nouns/deliverables; logic network = verbs/dependencies; Gantt = timeline; dependency = task that can't start until another finishes
  • Extra work requires a signed change order; building-design impacts go in an RFC
  • ANSI/InfoComm 10:2013 = performance verification/commissioning
PART V · CHAPTERS 20–22 · SERVICING AV SOLUTIONS

Servicing AV Solutions

Operations upkeep, maintenance, and troubleshooting. Troubleshooting/repair ≈ 10% of your score — and it's nearly all one method.

The systematic method with its exits: simple checks can end it early; divide-and-conquer loops until the fault is isolated; every fix gets verified and documented.

Concept Essentials

Warranty taxonomy

Manufacturer's warranty: new device fails in the first weeks. System warranty: the integrator's coverage of the installed system. Service agreement: commits the AV company to ongoing preventive maintenance.

Preventive maintenance

Fix it before it fails: replacing a projector lamp near end-of-life on schedule is the canonical example. Upgrades are not maintenance.

First response to a problem report

Gather symptom information first — before traveling, before touching anything. Working at all? Powers on? Error messages? All sources or one? This narrows causes and shapes what you bring.

Most likely culprit

Cables and connectors are the most common AV failure point. Next step in a dead-input chain with verified signal and correct input: swap the cable for a known-good one.

Intermittent problems

Defined as failures that are difficult to reproduce. Either observe until it recurs or have the client call immediately when it does — and ask demarcation questions (before/after what?).

User error counts

Always consider it — users change settings and take actions that cause failures. Distinguish abnormal performance from misunderstood normal performance.

Updates cascade

An update to one element (including owner-furnished computers/network) can trigger chains of unexpected results in connected systems. Consider carefully before installing.

Operations upkeep (Ch. 20)

Stock management, equipment security and theft deterrence, up-to-date equipment records, staff training, client-site monitoring. Procedural, lightly tested — know it exists.

EXAM TIP Troubleshooting questions are usually "what's the NEXT logical step?" Answer with the method: symptoms → simple checks → halve the system → swap known-good. The wrong answers skip steps (replace parts immediately) or regress (re-ask the user after diagnosis has begun).

Part V — Most Tested

  • First troubleshooting step: clearly identify the failure symptoms
  • Most efficient localization: divide the system in half, repeat on the failed half
  • Most likely failure source: cable/connector; confirm by swapping known-good
  • Intermittent = difficult to reproduce; user error is always a candidate
  • Manufacturer's warranty covers early failures; service agreement = ongoing preventive maintenance
  • Preventive maintenance = scheduled replacement before failure (lamps, filters)
  • First response to any client problem report: gather symptom detail before traveling
